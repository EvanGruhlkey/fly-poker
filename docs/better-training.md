# Mixed poker policy and public betting history

The browser samples the model's legal-action probabilities at temperature 1. It subtracts the largest logit before softmax to avoid overflow. It adds no independent action noise. A seeded random source makes the sampler testable. The previous greedy rule chose twice-pot in every saved probe even when that action had only 53% to 70% probability.

The new `cards-history-v2` encoder has 250 features. The first 118 retain the original own-card and public-table representation. The remaining 132 encode the last 12 public betting actions in chronological order. Each action contains two actor indicators relative to the observing player, four street indicators, four action-kind indicators, and the incremental chip payment divided by 800. Calls record the amount actually paid, including capped all-in calls. Checks pay zero. Blind posting and settlement are represented by table features rather than betting actions. Neither encoder includes the opponent's cards or the deck.

An explicit migration preserves the old graph, connection strengths, neuron dynamics, policy head, and first 118 sensory columns. It initializes the 132 new sensory columns to zero. A parity test verifies that migration preserves the old logits and values even with nonzero history inputs. Migration starts a new optimizer and progress counter. Normal checkpoint loading rejects the older observation schema. Version 3 checkpoints use a separate Modal run directory. The browser accepts both released schemas and chooses the encoder using verified model metadata, so the previous release remains usable until the replacement is verified.

The imitation teacher estimates equity with 64 unknown-card samples instead of 16. It applies importance weights from a preflop hand-strength prior conditioned on the opponent's public aggression. This prior is a heuristic, not a fitted opponent range. The action targets are a soft distribution over incremental showdown values. Already committed chips are sunk. The teacher excludes unmatched opponent wagers that return after a short all-in call. It assumes the opponent calls a raise and models no future street decisions or fold equity. Those assumptions can favor large bets with an equity advantage. The teacher is not a poker solver.

Warm training selects observations evenly across all four streets at 100 BB. Actor-critic training uses 100 BB for the first 128 updates, then chooses 100 BB in 75% of hands and unequal stacks in 25%. The opponent mixture retains random, passive, equity, and frozen current-policy opponents. Checkpoints resume exactly, including the curriculum stage.

Evaluation compares the newly trained sampled policy with the migrated starting policy. Both play matched held-out 100-BB deals against random and equity opponents, with seats swapped. Per-hand policy and opponent random seeds match between candidates. The report includes action counts by street and paired bootstrap intervals for the return difference. A small pilot cannot establish poker strength. Sampling restores mixed behavior without proving that training improved the strategy.
